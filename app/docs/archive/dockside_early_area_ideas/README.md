## Dockside

- If you held any hope for any change upon your return, it was held foolishly.
- Rain slicks the planks underfoot. The docks breathe and creak like a tired animal.
- Lantern light wobbles across dark water, never quite settling.
- watching nothing in particular.
- Examine the pile of ropes by the water.
- Walk closer to the water’s edge.
- Speak to the Dockmaster.
- Try the door to the boathouse.
- Approach the longhouse at the end of the pier.
- Leave the docks and follow the track inland toward the fields.
- You’ve already taken what was there. The ropes are just ropes again.
- You shift the coils aside and something dull glints in the lantern light.
- The ropes are heavy with salt and rain. Nothing but wet fibre and splinters.
- The tide laps softly against the posts. Something moves beneath the surface — or you imagine it does.
- The Dockmaster stares at you sullenly.
- Murder is in his heart, and heading towards his hands.
- The Dockmaster doesn’t look at you this time. He just keeps watching the water.
- The Dockmaster eyes you without turning his head.
- “Docks are closed,” he says. “Been that way all night.”
- You seem out-of-sorts. Anything I can do to help?
- “Closed to who?”
- Ask about recent trouble.
- Leave him to his watch.
- He snorts. “To anyone smart enough to stay out of the water.”
- He hesitates, then spits into the sea.
- “Things go missing. Sometimes they come back wrong.”
- “Ach…” He scrapes a thumb under his nose.
- “Lost my lucky earring this last tenday. Can’t seem to unearth it.”
- This earring?
- Ah, that’s too bad.
- The Dockmaster’s head snaps toward you.
- “Storm god’s bollocks! Where did ye find it?”
- He squints, then waves the question away with a ragged breath.
- “Ah — it matters not. Willing to part with it for 25 gold?”
- Yes.
- No.
- He snatches it like it might vanish again.
- “Good. Good. Now take your coin and go.”
- His face hardens.
- “Curses — get away from me then.”
- The boathouse door hangs crooked in its frame, swollen shut by damp.
- Force the lock.
- Step away from the door.
- The lock gives with a muted crack.
- The lock scrapes, but holds. Your fingers ache in the cold.
- The longhouse looms, dark windows staring back at you.
- No light within. No sound.
- Whatever waits inside has patience.
- Push the door open.
- Think better of it and turn back.

## Boathouse

- The boathouse smells of rot and old river mud.
- Water drips steadily from a cracked beam into a black puddle on the floor.
- Inspect the skiff.
- The cut is deliberate. Whatever did this wanted the boat loose.
- Search the back of the boathouse.
- You find nothing of value — only more questions.
- Leave the boathouse and return to the docks.

## Longhouse

- The door groans open.
- Inside, the longhouse is hollow and still. Benches lie overturned.
- Something has scraped deep grooves into the floorboards.
- This place is not empty.
- But it is not ready.
- Step back out into the rain.
